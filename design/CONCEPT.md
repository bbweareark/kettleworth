# Kettleworth — concept

Written before any gallery was opened, so the picks come from this product rather than habit.

## 1. What this product is like, physically

**A training sheet on a clipboard.** The thing a coach hands you at the door: a printed card,
ruled into rows, one line per exercise, columns for set, weight, reps and effort, with the
week stamped at the top and your own pencil marks down the margin. Not a cinema, not a
magazine, not a gallery. A working document you hold in a gym.

## 2. Tone

**Exacting · plainspoken · industrial.**
It is **not cinematic.** The previous build was a darkroom product editorial — moody
photography, oversized display type, the app presented as an artifact under a spotlight. That
is a poster for a training app, not a training app. This one is the sheet itself.

## 3. The one memorable idea

**The whole app is one continuous ruled sheet.** Every surface is numbered rows separated by
hairline rules, never cards. The row is the atom: on Today it is an exercise, on Progress it
is a lift, on Nutrition it is a meal. You always know where you are because the margin numbers
keep counting. A logbook you can scroll.

## 4. Ground and light

**Cool drafting grey, ink-dark type, flat.** No warm paper, no vellum, no parchment, no
near-black, no midnight — four of the six apps in this portfolio already live on warm paper
and two on a dark ground.

Flat surfaces, no elevation, no soft shadow. Depth comes from **rules**: hairline, half, and
one heavy rule that carries the eye. One light source only where a real photograph needs it.

## 5. What imagery is real here

The product's own data, set as type: tables of sets, reps, loads and weeks. Real exercise
photography exists in the library and stays there, cropped hard and used at full bleed when a
section needs a breath. **No hero portrait of the app as an object.** The hero must be
fillable by a table, because the table is what the product actually is.
